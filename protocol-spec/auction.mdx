## Overview

The Auction Manager is a centralized component that coordinates the selection of Solvers for user swap intents. The process works as follows:

1. **Solver Registration**: Solvers register with the Auction Manager and advertise their availability for specific chains and assets.
2. **Quote Request**: When a user initiates a swap intent via the dApp, the Auction Manager requests quotes from eligible Solvers.
3. **Winner Selection**: The Auction Manager evaluates quotes based on criteria such as price, speed, and reputation, then selects the best Solver as the winner.
4. **User Lock**: The user derives a secret and locks funds on the source chain, naming the winning Solver.
5. **Solver Lock & Secret Transmission**: The winning Solver locks funds on the destination chain. The user transmits the derived secret to the Solver, who then redeems on both chains. The Auction Manager enforces reliability through reputation tracking and slashing mechanisms.

## Auction Flow (On-Chain)

<Steps>
  <Step title="User Derives Secret and Locks">
    The user derives a secret using the [recoverable secret generation mechanism](/protocol/atomic-swaps-secret_gen) and locks funds on the source chain by calling the `lock()` function with the hashlock of that secret, specifying the winning Solver's address and a timelock that defines the swap duration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Solver Locks">
    The winning Solver observes the `TokenLocked` event and locks equivalent funds on the destination chain by calling the `lock()` function with the same hashlock and intent ID. The Solver provides a reward amount to incentivize timely redemption.
  </Step>
  <Step title="User Transmits Secret">
    The user observes the Solver's lock on the destination chain and transmits the derived secret to the Solver via off-chain channels.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Redemption & Completion">
    The Solver reveals the secret on the destination chain to unlock the user's funds (earning a reward for this action), and on the source chain to claim their primary fee. The Solver is economically incentivized to complete both redemptions quickly—if they delay on the destination chain, another actor can step in and claim the reward instead. If the Solver fails to act reliably, the Auction Manager applies penalties (reputation loss, slashing).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Auction Manager Responsibilities

- **Solver Registration & Tracking**: Maintains a registry of active Solvers and tracks their reputation scores and performance history.
- **Quote Evaluation**: Receives swap intents and requests competitive quotes from eligible Solvers on the required chains.
- **Winner Selection**: Selects the best Solver based on price, latency, and reputation metrics.
- **Reliability Enforcement**: Monitors Solver performance (on-time locks, timely redemptions, fund releases) and applies slashing based on behavior.
- **Intent Settlement**: Confirms swap completion or failure and updates Solver reputation accordingly.

## Solver Reliability & Incentives

The Auction Manager enforces Solver reliability through:

- **Reputation Score**: Each Solver maintains a reputation score that influences future quote acceptance and auction selection.
- **Reward/Slashing Mechanism**: Solvers who fail to lock, redeem, or release user funds are penalized through slashing; other Solvers can earn rewards by completing the swap on the failed Solver's behalf.
- **Auction Selection Bias**: The Auction Manager favors Solvers with higher reputation scores and progressively reduces winning probability for unreliable Solvers.

For details on how rewards and slashing work on-chain, see [Reward-Slashing](/protocol-spec/reward-slashing).
